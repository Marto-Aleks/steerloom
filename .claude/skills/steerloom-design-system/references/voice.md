# Voice & copy

Steerloom sounds like a sharp, calm delivery lead: direct, specific, never hypey, never blaming.

## Principles
1. **Outcomes over features.** "Know when work will land" beats "Monte Carlo forecasting module".
2. **Specific over vague.** Real numbers, real names, real dates. "9 days in progress, 3× the usual" beats "this item is taking long".
3. **Contrast to sharpen.** "X, not Y": "Decisions, not noise." "Dates with confidence, not guesses."
4. **Short and confident.** Fragments are fine for headings and trust points: "Read-only. Always."
5. **Honest about uncertainty.** Forecasts state confidence. Never "guaranteed", "always on time", "AI magic".
6. **No blame.** Describe the work, not the people: "Review is holding 6 items", not "Reviewers are slow".

## Volume differences
- **Loud:** headings promise or take a stance ("Six numbers that tell the truth about your board"). Lead paragraphs ≤ 25 words.
- **Focused:** headings state the situation ("2 things need you today"). Findings follow what + evidence + so-what.
- **Quiet:** labels are plain nouns; help text explains in one sentence; errors say how to fix.

## Vocabulary
Use: board, items, work, stage, briefing, signal, at risk, bottleneck, aging, rework, usual limit, forecast, confidence, land/ship.
Avoid: tickets (except when quoting a tool), velocity, story points, KPIs, synergy, leverage, AI-powered, revolutionary, seamless, unlock.

## Patterns
- Buttons: verb + object — "Connect my board", "Export briefing", "See the items".
- Empty states: outcome headline + one sentence + one action. "Your first briefing is a click away. Connect a board and Steerloom reads its history in about five minutes."
- Errors: what happened + what to do. "We couldn't reach your board. Check the URL or reconnect."
- Confirmations: past tense, brief. "Board connected." "Saved."
- Numbers: use digits, include units, "3×" for multiples, dates as "Nov 14".
- Sentence case for all headings and buttons.
